Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 October 2026

Solace Tree is a private grief journal for iPhone. What you write about someone you have lost belongs to you. This policy explains, in plain words, what stays on your iPhone, what leaves it and why, how long anything is kept, and how to delete it.

In short

  • What you write, record and keep in Solace Tree stays on your iPhone, in encrypted storage. We cannot read it.
  • Only if you subscribe to Premium is a copy kept in cloud backup, in the European Union, so it can come back on a new iPhone. When Premium ends, that copy is deleted 90 days later.
  • Your check-ins, every mood and feeling you record, and the faith you choose never leave your iPhone, not even with Premium.
  • Your anonymous sign-in, and usage analytics if you switch them on, are handled by Google, which processes them in the United States as well.
  • No ads, no tracking, and no selling or sharing of your information for advertising. Usage analytics stay off unless you switch them on. Crash reports never include what you write, and you can switch them off when you first open Solace Tree or at any time later.
  • You can export or delete everything yourself, from inside the app, at any time.

Who is responsible

Solace Tree is published by Can Kanbur, the controller of the processing described here. You can reach us at cankanbur1@gmail.com.

What stays on your iPhone

Everything you put into Solace Tree is stored on your iPhone:

  • the people you remember: their names, your relationship, photos, and dates of birth and passing;
  • memories: words, photos, videos and voice recordings;
  • journal entries and letters;
  • check-ins (how you are feeling, with an optional note) and breathing sessions;
  • important dates and the plans you make for them;
  • your safety plan;
  • the answers you give when you start, such as your relationship to the person, how you are feeling and whether you would like spiritual support;
  • your settings, reminders and reading progress.

Words and records are kept in an encrypted database whose key is held in the iOS Keychain. Photos, videos and recordings are kept in the app’s private storage, protected by iOS. Reminders are scheduled by your iPhone itself; we do not send notifications from a server. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, that backup can include Solace Tree’s data; it is handled by Apple under Apple’s own terms.

What you export — keepsakes, JSON or CSV exports, and backup files, which you can protect with a password — is created on your iPhone and goes only where you choose to send or save it. We never receive it.

Your account

When you finish the first steps, Solace Tree creates an anonymous account with Firebase Authentication, a Google service. It is a random identifier: no name, email address, phone number or password. It is what your purchases and, if you use it, your cloud backup belong to. Firebase Authentication runs only in Google’s data centres in the United States, so your account’s identifier and sign-in records are processed there.

You can choose to continue with Apple, so your account can move with you to a new iPhone. Solace Tree does not ask Apple for your name or email address; Apple gives us an identifier that is unique to Solace Tree.

Stored with your account are its identifier and creation date; the technical sign-in records Google keeps to protect sign-ins (the IP address and device information of a request, which Google keeps for a few weeks); and the history of your privacy choices — the version of the privacy notice you were shown and what you chose on it, such as analytics and crash reports, with the time of each choice. We keep that history because we must be able to show what you agreed to. It is deleted with your account.

Solace Tree does not send marketing.

Premium cloud backup

Only while you have an active Premium subscription, Solace Tree keeps a copy of your content in the cloud, so it can be restored on another iPhone signed in to the same account. The copy includes your profile (the name you entered, your photo, three answers from the first steps — your relationship to the person, how long ago the loss was and the kind of support you prefer — and when you last checked in), the people you remember, memories with their photos, videos and voice recordings, journal entries and letters without their moods, important dates, breathing history, reading progress and app preferences.

Some things never leave your iPhone, even with Premium: your check-ins, every mood and feeling you record, the faith you choose, your other answers from the first steps, your safety plan and unfinished drafts. Premium includes 250 MB of cloud storage for photos, videos and voice recordings. A file that does not fit, or is larger than 50 MB, stays only on your iPhone, and so does a voice note added to a photo or a written memory.

The copy is stored with Google Cloud — Cloud Firestore and Cloud Storage for Firebase — in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany), where our server functions run too. It is encrypted in transit and at rest, and access rules allow only your account to read it. It is not end-to-end encrypted: as with most cloud services, the provider’s systems can technically reach stored data. We do not look at it.

  • Something you delete is marked as deleted straight away and removed for good after 30 days. Our backups, which exist to undo an accident on our side, can hold it for up to eight days more: seven days of point-in-time recovery, and a daily backup kept for seven days. Files that no longer belong to a memory are removed by a daily clean-up.
  • Breathing history older than three years is removed from the cloud copy.
  • When Premium ends, nothing new is uploaded (deletions still are). 90 days after Premium ends, the cloud copy, with its files, is deleted automatically. What is on your iPhone is not affected.

Purchases

Purchases are made through Apple, and we never see your payment details. To know what you have bought, Solace Tree uses RevenueCat (United States). RevenueCat receives your anonymous account identifier, the details of your purchases from Apple (which product, when, renewals and any free trial), and the technical details needed to validate them, such as the app version and your App Store country.

Our backend keeps a record of what your account is entitled to — for example whether Premium is active and until when — which decides whether cloud backup is available. Messages from RevenueCat about purchases are logged for 30 days so that none is processed twice.

Crash reports

When Solace Tree runs into an error, it sends a crash report to Sentry, stored in the European Union (Frankfurt). A report contains technical details: the error and where in the code it happened, the app version, the device model and iOS version, whether the account is anonymous or signed in with Apple, and a random identifier for this installation of Solace Tree, which is not linked to your account. A small sample of timing measurements helps us find slow screens. Reports never contain what you write, your account identifier, your name or your email address; screenshots and screen recordings are off, and IP addresses are not stored. Errors in our server functions are reported the same way, without the content of any request.

Crash reports are on by default, because they keep the app working. You can switch them off on the privacy screen when you first open Solace Tree, or at any time in Profile → Privacy & Security → Crash reports. Switching them off stops them at once, except reports of a crash of the app itself, which stop from the next launch.

Usage analytics, only if you say yes

If you switch on anonymous usage analytics — when you first open Solace Tree, or later in Profile → Privacy & Security — Solace Tree sends events to Google Analytics for Firebase (Google) about which features are used: for example that a journal entry was saved, a keepsake template was chosen, the Premium screen was opened or a purchase was completed. Events carry details that identify no one, such as counts, word counts or the name of a template. Never what you write, never names, and never how you feel: a check-in is counted without its mood, and no event says that a check-in was a difficult one.

While analytics are on, Google Analytics also records a few things by itself: that the app was opened for the first time, that a session started or the app was updated, how long it was in use, and App Store purchases with their product and price. These come with the device model, iOS version, app version and language. They stop when you switch analytics off; they cannot be switched off on their own.

Events are linked to a random identifier Google Analytics creates on your iPhone, never to your account. Solace Tree does not use the advertising identifier at all and never asks to track you. Google uses your IP address only to work out a rough location — country and city — and then discards it; for people in the European Union, the United Kingdom and Switzerland this happens on servers there. Google signals and personalised advertising are off, and event-level data is kept for two months. Analytics stay off until you switch them on; switching them off stops them at once and deletes the identifier on your iPhone.

App updates

Solace Tree can download small updates to its own code through Expo (EAS Update, United States). These requests carry technical information such as the app version and platform, never your content.

Settings from this website

When it starts, Solace Tree reads a small settings file from this website: the oldest version of the app that still works, and the limits for cloud storage. The request carries no identifier and nothing about you, but like any visit to the website it reaches GitHub, which hosts it (see This website).

What we never do

  • No advertising and no advertising networks.
  • No tracking across other companies’ apps or websites. Solace Tree never asks for permission to track you.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your information for advertising.
  • No use of your journal, memories or recordings to train AI models.

Why we may process it

  • Running Solace Tree and Premium for you — your account, cloud backup and purchases — is necessary to provide the service you asked for.
  • The answers that say most about your health and your beliefs — your check-ins, every mood and feeling you record, and the faith you choose — stay on your iPhone and are never in the cloud copy. If you write about your health or beliefs yourself, in a memory, a journal entry or a letter, Premium backs that text up like everything else you write, only to keep it for you: we never read, analyse or use it for anything else, and you can delete it at any time.
  • Usage analytics rely on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time.
  • Crash reports, sign-in records and checking that requests come from Solace Tree rely on our legitimate interest in a working, secure app. You can object by switching crash reports off.
  • Keeping records of consent and purchases is a legal obligation.

Deleting your data and your account

In Solace Tree, go to Profile → Delete Account. After you confirm — and, if you continued with Apple, confirm once more with Apple — Solace Tree:

  1. asks Apple to revoke Solace Tree’s Sign in with Apple access, if you used it;
  2. deletes your photos, videos and recordings from cloud storage;
  3. deletes your customer record and purchase history at RevenueCat;
  4. deletes your account, and with it everything stored for it: the cloud copy, your privacy choices and your entitlement record;
  5. erases Solace Tree’s data on your iPhone.

Deleting your account does not cancel an App Store subscription: cancel it first in Settings → your name → Subscriptions. Apple keeps its own record of your purchases. One small thing stays on your iPhone: when you first opened Solace Tree and when it last asked for an App Store rating, so it does not ask again too soon. It contains nothing you wrote.

You can also delete single entries, memories or people at any time; they leave the cloud copy as described above.

Your rights

You have the right to access your information, to correct it, to delete it, to take it with you, to restrict or object to its processing, and to withdraw your consent at any time. Most of this you can do yourself: Profile → Privacy & Security → Export My Data gives you everything in a portable file, and Delete Account erases it. For anything else, write to cankanbur1@gmail.com; we answer within one month. You can also complain to a data protection authority, such as the one where you live — in Türkiye, the Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK).

Security

Your content is encrypted on your iPhone. Everything sent to our providers travels over encrypted connections. Our database lets each account read only its own records, and our server functions never write what they receive to their logs. Our server answers only the real Solace Tree app: each request carries a short-lived token from Firebase App Check, which your iPhone obtains with Apple’s App Attest. The token says nothing about you or your content, and Google does not keep what your iPhone sends to obtain it.

Children

Solace Tree is written for adults. It is not directed at children under 16, and we do not knowingly collect their information. If you believe a child has used Solace Tree, contact us and we will delete it.

This website

This website is hosted by GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other sites. GitHub may keep technical logs of visits, such as IP addresses, for security. The /get/ page only forwards you to the App Store. Solace Tree itself reads one file from this website when it starts (see Settings from this website).
